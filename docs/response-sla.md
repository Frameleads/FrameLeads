# Response SLA Engine V1

Response SLAs are durable, tenant-owned deadlines for **human-owned revenue replies**. They do not send messages, approve Decisions, override the Sales Constitution, or change Automation eligibility. All timestamps are stored in UTC and the V1 policy uses elapsed wall-clock time, including nights and weekends.

The tenant's `ResponseSLAPolicy` defaults to enabled with durations of CRITICAL 15 minutes, HIGH 60, MEDIUM 240, LOW 1440, and UNKNOWN 240. These are FrameLeads product defaults, not an industry benchmark. Due-soon is derived after 75% of the duration. First-breach assignment escalation is enabled by default; tenants can change the bounded durations, due-soon percentage, switches, and policy revision through authenticated settings. Disabling the policy cancels unresolved SLAs on their next refresh and preserves history.

One `ResponseSLAInstance` belongs to each applicable Decision. Applicability is centralized: HUMAN_APPROVAL, MANDATORY_ESCALATION, NEEDS_REVIEW, or uncertain execution can create a human-response SLA. Confirmed suppression, confident non-revenue cases, blocked/held outbound actions, and successful Autopilot do not. The clock starts at the first persisted human-responsibility Automation resolution, the NEEDS_REVIEW Decision transition, or the failed execution completion. Lazy materialization uses those timestamps, so Inbox refresh cannot restart the clock. The first available Revenue-at-Risk band sets a fixed `dueAt`; unavailable risk uses UNKNOWN. The instance snapshots the starting risk score/band/confidence and policy revision. Later risk or policy changes do not silently move its deadline.

Before `dueAt`, status remains ACTIVE; due-soon is display-only. At or after `dueAt`, the first evaluation changes the instance to BREACHED and stores `breachedAt` once. If escalation is enabled, `escalationLevel` becomes 1, `escalationReason` records `SLA_BREACH`, and the existing `DecisionAssignment` is reused or a HUMAN_REVIEW assignment is created. Existing LEGAL, SALES_ENGINEERING, or other specialized queue and reason are preserved. Repeated evaluation does not create another assignment. There are no alerts, messages, manager chains, or breach timers.

A successfully sent governed action or intentional human REJECT resolves the SLA. ESCALATE and uncertain send failure leave it open. Authoritative suppression, a non-revenue classification, a no-longer-human-owned action, or policy disable cancels an unresolved SLA with a bounded reason. RESOLVED and CANCELLED are terminal and retain their original due and breach history. Automation remains the sole authority for execution.

FrameLeads has a weekly digest cron, but its cadence is unsuitable for minute-scale response deadlines; no new scheduled job is required by V1. Decision creation, governed-action responses, Inbox load, and the tenant-scoped SLA read endpoint synchronize relevant instances. Inbox work is batched in groups of 100 Decisions; it reads persisted Decision, Revenue-at-Risk, Automation, assignment, and suppression facts without loading Brain, Playbook, Constitution rules, full Memory, or conversation history. The read endpoint also reassesses risk deterministically. There are no AI calls, AI usage rows, external notifications, or real sends from SLA evaluation. Future Decision Replay can use the stored start, risk/policy snapshot, fixed due time, breach/escalation fields, and resolution/cancellation timestamps. Roadmap #9 still owns revenue prioritization; Roadmap #8 still owns action authority.
